# Per-seat token rate, first readings, 2026-10-14

`GET /throughput/seats` landed in [#3703](https://github.com/SageSeekerSociety/cheese/pull/3703), main `ea520a2a`, deployed the same day (`DEPLOY OK: sha=ea520a2 healthy`). These are the first readings taken from that endpoint, on dev, at 2026-10-14 17:38 UTC, from the build serving the platform then: `4386413f`, two commits past `ea520a2a` and containing it. The figures cover every seat on the platform; the room id in the call is the door the request comes through, not a filter.

The measure exists to keep "held back by a rate limit" apart from "idle". On the 24h window one Claude subscription seat read at roughly half its own 7d figure while it kept running — the reading it was built to produce.

## What the number is

A turn's `output_tokens` divided by the wall-clock seconds from its start to its stop, grouped by seat × model × supply, reported as p50 and p90. Window throughput (`sum(output_tokens) ÷ window seconds`) rides along on every row as `window_tps`.

The denominator is the whole turn, thinking and tool calls included, not the seconds spent emitting tokens: the ledger holds no moment finer than `created_at` and a turn carries only its start, its delivery and its stop, so a pure streaming rate cannot be recovered from it. The figure is lower than a harness streaming TPS on purpose. Rows with `output_tokens = 0` are dropped — the ledger writes one for a turn that recorded no usage at all, so the row marks usage that never arrived, not a turn that produced nothing. A seat-level row pools every model and supply that seat used; read the seat × model × supply rows for one comparison.

Across the 63 seat × model × supply rows in this window, the four on a Claude subscription sit between p50 14.4 and 32.6, while the 59 gateway rows span p50 0.036 to 711.7. A seat that runs both supplies appears twice: `cheese` is the `claude-opus-5-5` subscription row above and a gateway row at p50 711.7, which is not a faster seat but a different population of turns.

## The same three rows over longer windows

| seat / model / supply | 24h | 7d | 30d |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `cheesex-opus-cc` / `claude-opus-5-5` / subscription | 14.9 (32 turns) | 28.6 (275) | 34.8 (804) |
| `cheese-sonnet` / `claude-sonnet-5-5` / subscription | 29.8 (8) | 19.4 (43) | 19.4 (43) |
| `cheese` / `claude-opus-5-5` / subscription | 32.6 (21) | 37.2 (80) | 71.3 (373) |

`cheesex-opus-cc` reads at less than half its own 30d figure in the last day, with 32 turns and 259 calls in that day — it kept working and produced slower, which is the shape a rate limit takes here rather than the shape of idleness. The orchestrator's own review of the same morning (the project library's `总调度复盘 10-13 22:30–10-14 03:57`) records this seat, `cheese-sonnet` and `cheese-fable` as the three seats nine task lines were moved off at 03:33 UTC because they had cooled down; `cheese-sonnet` has since read above its own baseline.

## Reproduction

```sh
OKCHEESE_PASS=… python3 .claude/scripts/seat_tps.py \
  --topic 31095c05-3888-49a6-a448-2de40d1df78a --window 24h
```

That call is what the tables above hold: the same endpoint, on dev, at the same time. Re-running later moves the numbers — these are a snapshot, not a baseline to ratchet against.

## Limits

- One reading, taken platform-wide on dev. Nothing here estimates the platform's average or any seat's steady-state rate.
- p90 is inflated by turns whose recorded duration is near zero — a backfilled turn, or a clock jump. The p50 is the figure to read.
  **Superseded 2026-10-15:** neither column is readable — the pairing itself is wrong, not just its
  tail. See `seat-tps-2026-10-15.md`; both columns are gone from the endpoint.
- A seat's row pools every model and supply it used, so a seat that spread its turns over several models shows a median across all of them.
- An empty seat string is a turn no seat claimed: one the session started by itself, or one whose seat was gone before it could be attributed.
