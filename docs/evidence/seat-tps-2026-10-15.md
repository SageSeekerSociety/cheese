# The per-seat token rate: what the old figures paired up, and the reading that replaces them, 2026-10-15

`GET /throughput/seats` reported a **per-turn rate**: one turn's `output_tokens` divided by
that turn's start-to-stop seconds, grouped by seat × model × supply and reported as p50 and
p90. On the 24h window read at 2026-10-14 23:32 UTC, seat `cheese` showed p50 732.2725 and
p90 11857.9265 over 26,811,484 output tokens and 266,875.828 turn seconds — 100.5 token/s by
total-over-total. Nearly every row read like that. The figure did not mean what its column name
said, and it could not be made to mean it.

This note is the evidence, the reading that replaces it, and the two windows side by side. The
replacement is arithmetic over the same rows the endpoint was already returning
(`backend/app/domain/platform_stats/throughput.py`), so the new columns below are recomputed
from the same pull — no deploy was needed to produce them.

## Why one turn's tokens over one turn's seconds is not a rate

`resource_usage` is written by two paths, and only one of them knows which turn produced the
usage.

**`gateway`** rows come from `agent.gateway.drain_new_usage`: it reads the **project's virtual
key's daily cumulative totals per model**, subtracts the checkpoint stored in
`project.settings`, and returns the difference. `agent.gateway_usage.charge_turn_spend` then
charges that difference with `turn_id=turn_id` of the turn that was ending — and
`_schedule_deferred_drain` retries 20 seconds later with the **same** turn id. One key serves
every seat and every turn in the project, so a row's `output_tokens` is everything the key
spent since the last successful drain, stamped on whichever turn happened to be settling when
the drain succeeded. The denominator is that one turn's wall clock. A drain that failed for a
while, or that finalized several days at once (`_prior_days` finalizes every day since the
checkpoint), lands days of traffic on one turn.

**`subscription`** rows are written per call by `usage.subscription_ingest`, one row per
`/v1/messages` response, with `turn_id` from `WorkIndex.work_at` — the latest turn whose first
block started before the call's timestamp, within six hours. The numerator is attributed far
better than the gateway's, but the denominator still is not the time that turn spent
generating: a turn can hold parallel calls (subagents), so the turn's wall clock is a span the
calls share rather than a sum of call durations.

Neither half gives a number that pairs a turn's own tokens with a turn's own seconds. Averaging
over turns does not repair it: it takes the same bad pairing and reports its distribution.

## What the readings show

The tell is internal to the row. A row's `output_tokens` and its `calls` are read from the same
cumulative counters in the same drain, so `output_tokens ÷ calls` cannot exceed what one model
call can return. Rows over any single call's output ceiling are rows whose two counters do not
describe the same calls.

## Naming the offending turns is not possible from here

The rows above are groups. Pointing at the turns inside them would need each turn's token count,
and no endpoint exposes it: `/run-records/turns` returns per-turn start and stop with no usage,
and `/throughput/seats` is the only route that publishes `output_tokens`. The report stays at
the group level.

## The reading that replaces it

**`window_tps` = `output_tokens ÷ window seconds`** — the group's credited output per second of
the window, idle stretches included. Every figure it uses is read from one place for one span:
the tokens and the seconds are both the group's, over the window. It answers "how much is this
group producing per second" and nothing finer.

Two things to read alongside it, and one thing it cannot say:

- `turns` / `calls` separate "running but slow" from "not running": those two normal and
  `window_tps` low is a throttled or unlucky seat; a small `turns` is an idle one.
- `turn_seconds` stays as a background quantity — how much in-turn wall clock the group had.
  It is **not** a denominator any more.
- Per-seat credit on `gateway` is approximate by construction: the drain is stamped on whoever
  was settling, so seats blur into each other. The `model × supply` totals are the ledger's own
  numbers; the seat split is not.

## Limits

- Both windows are single readings, anchored on "now"; re-running moves them.
- The 30d figures cover the platform on dev, not production.
- `output_tokens = 0` marker rows are still excluded (see the module docstring), so a turn whose
  usage never arrived contributes nothing to any column.
- The figures cannot tell a fork/PR-branch seat from its parent: `agent_handle` is whatever the
  turn recorded.
- The old p50/p90 columns are kept in the tables above only so the change can be read against
  what it replaced. They are gone from the endpoint, the two table scripts and the tests.

## Reproduction

```sh
OKCHEESE_PASS=… python3 .claude/scripts/seat_tps.py \
  --topic 31095c05-3888-49a6-a448-2de40d1df78a --window 30d
```

The old columns were read from the same endpoint at 2026-10-14 23:32 UTC (24h) and the tables
above from 2026-10-15 03:19 UTC (both windows).
